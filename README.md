## 1. What Does It Do?

The Schengen Stay Calculator is a web-based application developed using the Laravel 13 framework. It is designed to help travellers monitor their time spent within the Schengen Area and calculate how many days they may have available for future visits.

It is based on the Schengen short-stay rule, commonly known as the **90 days in any rolling 180-day period rule**.

Under this rule, eligible third-country nationals visiting the Schengen Area for short stays may generally spend a maximum of 90 days within any 180-day period.

The application helps users:

- Record previous visits to the Schengen Area.
- Enter arrival and departure dates.
- Calculate the total number of days spent in the Schengen Area.
- Determine the remaining days available within the applicable 180-day reference period.
- Check whether previous and ongoing stays appear to comply with the 90/180-day rule.
- Plan future visits based on their travel history.
- Identify potential overstay situations.
- Monitor their travel history in one convenient place.

### Intended Users

The application is primarily intended for:

- Visa-exempt travellers visiting the Schengen Area.
- Holders of short-stay Schengen visas, subject to individual visa limitations.
- Frequent travellers who visit Europe several times a year.
- Travellers who wish to plan their return visits carefully.
- Individuals who want a convenient way to monitor their accumulated Schengen days.

The application is intended as a personal travel-planning and monitoring tool.

## 2. Important Disclaimer, Precautions and Limitations

### 2.1 Advisory Tool Only

**This application is an advisory and informational tool. It does not provide legal advice, immigration authorisation, a visa, a residence permit or any legal exemption from Schengen immigration requirements.**

The results generated by this application are estimates based on the travel information entered by the user and the general 90/180-day calculation methodology.

A calculation indicating that a traveller has remaining days does not automatically mean that the traveller has a legal right to enter or remain in the Schengen Area.

The competent immigration and border authorities of the relevant Schengen countries retain the authority to determine:

- Whether a traveller satisfies the conditions for entry.
- Whether a traveller has complied with the applicable immigration rules.
- Whether a visa or other authorisation is required.
- Whether a traveller is permitted to remain for a particular period.
- Whether an overstay or immigration violation has occurred.

The application must therefore not be treated as an official determination of immigration status.

### 2.2 Accuracy of Information

The accuracy of the results depends on the accuracy and completeness of the information entered by the user.

Users are responsible for ensuring that:

- All previous Schengen visits are recorded.
- Arrival and departure dates are correct.
- No relevant travel period is omitted.
- Dates are entered in the correct format.
- Any special visa or residence-permit conditions are considered separately.

Incorrect or incomplete travel information may produce misleading results.

### 2.3 Special Visa Conditions

The general 90/180-day rule does not necessarily represent the full period authorised by an individual visa.

For example, a short-stay visa may authorise fewer than 90 days or impose a specific period of validity.

In such circumstances, the visa sticker and its conditions take precedence over the general maximum calculated by this application.

Users should independently verify:

- Visa validity dates.
- Number of entries permitted.
- Number of authorised days of stay.
- Territorial restrictions.
- Any other conditions attached to their visa.

### 2.4 Residence Permits and Long-Stay Visas

Periods of stay authorised under a valid residence permit or a national long-stay visa (Type D) are generally excluded from the short-stay 90/180-day calculation.

Such periods should not be entered as ordinary short-stay visits when calculating the general allowance.

However, users should check their individual immigration circumstances and applicable national rules.

### 2.5 No Guarantee of Entry

Even where the calculator indicates that a traveller has sufficient remaining days, entry may still be refused if other applicable entry conditions are not satisfied.

These may include:

- Valid travel documentation.
- Visa requirements.
- Purpose and conditions of the intended visit.
- Sufficient financial means.
- Other applicable immigration and border requirements.

**Always consult official immigration authorities before making important travel decisions.**

### 2.6 Official Reference

The calculation methodology is based on Article 6 of Regulation (EU) 2016/399 (Schengen Borders Code), as applicable.

Official resources:

- [European Commission – Short-stay calculator](https://home-affairs.ec.europa.eu/policies/schengen/border-crossing/short-stay-calculator_en)

## 7. How the Calculations Are Done

### 7.1 Understanding the 90/180-Day Rule

The general short-stay rule permits a maximum stay of:

**90 days within any rolling 180-day period.**

The important word is *any*.

The calculation does not operate on:

- A calendar year.
- A January-to-June period.
- A July-to-December period.
- A fixed six-month period beginning on the first arrival date.

Instead, the application must consider a moving 180-day reference window.

For every day being assessed, it looks backwards over the relevant 180 calendar days and counts the days spent in the Schengen Area.

The total must not exceed 90 days.

### 7.2 Entry and Exit Dates Are Inclusive

Both the date of arrival and the date of departure count as days of stay.

For example:

| Event | Date |
|---|---|
| Entry | 1 January |
| Exit | 10 January |
| Total days | 10 days |

The calculation is:

10 January - 1 January + 1 = 10 days.

This is important because excluding either the arrival or departure date would produce an incorrect result.

### 7.3 The Rolling 180-Day Calculation

For any selected date, the calculator determines the applicable reference period.

The basic formula is:

**Reference Period = Selected Date minus 179 calendar days through Selected Date**

The selected date is included, giving a total of 180 calendar days.

The calculator then:

1. Identifies all recorded Schengen stays overlapping the reference period.
2. Determines the days falling within that reference period.
3. Counts each qualifying calendar day only once.
4. Calculates the total number of days spent in the Schengen Area.
5. Compares that total with the maximum permitted 90 days.

The general calculation is:

`Remaining Days = 90 - Days Used`

For example:

| Description | Days |
|---|---:|
| Maximum permitted stay | 90 |
| Previous days used | 65 |
| Remaining allowance | 25 |

In this example, the traveller has 25 days of theoretical remaining allowance in the relevant reference period.

This does not necessarily mean that the traveller can immediately spend all 25 days consecutively. The calculation must be repeated for every subsequent day of the proposed stay.

### 7.4 Why the Calculation Must Be Repeated Daily

The 180-day reference period changes as time passes.

Older days of stay eventually fall outside the reference period, making those days available again, subject to the overall rule.

Consider the following example:

A traveller spends 90 consecutive days in the Schengen Area and then leaves.

After 90 uninterrupted days outside the Schengen Area, the earlier 90-day stay has fallen outside the relevant reference period.

The traveller may then generally become eligible for another stay of up to 90 days, subject to all other applicable requirements.

This is why the calculator must evaluate the rolling period rather than simply subtracting the total number of days ever spent in Europe from 90.

### 7.5 Multiple Visits

Where a traveller makes several visits, all qualifying days must be considered together.

Example:

| Visit | Entry | Exit | Days |
|---|---|---|---:|
| 1 | 1 January | 10 January | 10 |
| 2 | 1 March | 20 March | 20 |
| 3 | 1 May | 30 May | 30 |
| **Total** | | | **60** |

If all these days fall within the relevant 180-day reference period:

90 - 60 = 30 remaining days.

The traveller therefore has 30 days of theoretical remaining allowance for that particular reference date.

### 7.6 Future Travel Planning

For a planned future visit, the calculator must assess the previous travel history and the proposed stay together.

It should not simply calculate:

`90 - Previous Days Used`

Instead, it must evaluate the number of days spent in the Schengen Area for every day of the proposed future stay.

A traveller might have 30 days available on the proposed entry date, but the actual maximum continuous stay may be affected by the movement of the 180-day reference window and the dates of previous visits.

The correct planning method is therefore:

1. Select the proposed entry date.
2. Identify the previous 179 calendar days and the entry date itself.
3. Calculate the existing days of stay within that window.
4. Add the proposed entry date as a day of stay.
5. Check that the total does not exceed 90.
6. Move forward one calendar day.
7. Repeat the calculation for each additional proposed day.
8. Stop when the next day would cause the applicable limit to be exceeded.

The result is the maximum theoretical continuous stay under the general 90/180-day rule, assuming no other travel or legal restrictions apply.

### 7.7 Calculation Logic – Simplified Pseudocode

The following illustrates the basic daily checking process:

```javascript
function calculateDaysUsed(checkDate, previousStays) {
    const referenceStart = new Date(checkDate);

    referenceStart.setDate(
        referenceStart.getDate() - 179
    );

    let daysUsed = 0;

    for (
        let day = new Date(referenceStart);
        day <= checkDate;
        day.setDate(day.getDate() + 1)
    ) {
        for (const stay of previousStays) {
            if (
                day >= stay.entryDate &&
                day <= stay.exitDate
            ) {
                daysUsed++;
                break;
            }
        }
    }

    return daysUsed;
}
```

This is illustrative pseudocode for the core counting principle, not a complete production implementation. A production implementation should also normalise dates, handle ongoing stays, validate entry/exit pairs, prevent overlapping periods from being counted twice, and handle time zones consistently.

### 7.8 Important Calculation Considerations

A reliable implementation should observe the following:

**A. No double counting**

If two recorded travel periods overlap, the same calendar day must not be counted twice.

**B. Missing dates**

An entry without a corresponding exit, or an exit without a valid entry, should be flagged for correction rather than silently producing a potentially misleading result.

**C. Ongoing stays**

If a traveller is currently in the Schengen Area, the ongoing visit must be counted through the date being assessed.

**D. Date boundaries**

The arrival and departure dates must both be included.

**E. Historical records**

Travel dates outside the relevant 180-day period generally do not contribute to the current calculation, although they may become relevant when assessing an earlier date.

**F. Multiple Schengen countries**

All qualifying stays in Schengen countries must be aggregated into one calculation.

**G. Legal exceptions and special arrangements**

Certain nationalities, bilateral agreements, visa conditions and individual immigration circumstances may require separate assessment. The general calculation must not be assumed to cover every possible legal arrangement.
